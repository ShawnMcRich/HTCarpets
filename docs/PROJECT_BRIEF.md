# Project Brief

## Project

Hosseintalab digital commerce and brand transformation

## Owner

Shahin Ghanizadeh

## Business context

Hosseintalab is a multigenerational handwoven-carpet business based in Tehran's carpet bazaar. The business is reputable in specialist, collectible, antique, and export markets but has not previously operated a coordinated digital presence.

The immediate commercial opportunity is to make the family's expertise useful to ordinary domestic buyers: apartment owners, couples, families, and interior professionals looking for a handwoven carpet for a real home.

## Problem statement

Buying a handwoven carpet is difficult for a non-specialist. Customers may not understand market terminology, cannot easily judge authenticity or condition, and are uncertain about size, color, price, delivery, and whether a carpet will suit their home.

The business needs a Persian-first digital experience that converts offline reputation into understandable evidence and helps customers move confidently between online discovery, consultation, and the Tehran store.

## Product objective

Create a distinctive, trustworthy digital storefront and supporting content system that:

- presents real handwoven carpets for everyday residential use;
- explains carpet terminology without oversimplifying it;
- supports discovery by room, size, weaving origin, and pattern;
- makes expert consultation a primary conversion path;
- connects online browsing with nationwide delivery and store visits;
- preserves the family's antique and export heritage as evidence of expertise.

## Initial market

- Geography: Iran, nationwide
- Language and interface direction: Persian-first, RTL
- Primary users: apartment owners, couples, families, and home furnishers
- Secondary users: interior designers and architects
- Initial product focus: handwoven residential carpets and rugs

## Scope

### In scope

- Brand direction and digital identity application
- Persian brand voice and customer-facing copy
- Product and catalog taxonomy
- Responsive storefront experience
- Product, room, consultation, and expertise content
- Photography and short-video direction
- Consultation, store-visit, and online-purchase journeys
- Technical foundation, QA, analytics planning, and launch preparation
- Jira delivery management and portfolio evidence

### Not yet in scope

- Final payment and shipping integrations
- Production inventory system
- Final legal and return policies
- A verified public founding year
- Final vector master of the logo
- Antique and international commerce as primary launch channels

## Current deliverable

A responsive React and TypeScript storefront prototype with:

- a Persian-first home page;
- room-based discovery;
- real carpet taxonomy by size, origin, and pattern;
- category-image previews;
- representative product presentation;
- responsive header, footer, and mobile navigation;
- consultation and trust pathways;
- production-reference imagery;
- locally bundled typography and production build tooling.

## Success measures

The numerical targets remain to be approved. The measurement framework should include:

- consultation starts;
- product-detail and price inquiries;
- store-visit requests;
- completed orders;
- inquiry-to-order conversion;
- time from inquiry to recommendation;
- catalog accuracy and sold-item update time;
- delivery issues and return rate;
- qualified Instagram and organic-search traffic.

## Constraints and risks

- Final business history and founding claims require verification.
- Inventory and photography capacity have not yet been confirmed.
- Payment, shipping, return, and insurance policies remain open.
- The current logo is an exact raster reference, not a production vector master.
- Generated visual references must be replaced by photographs of actual inventory before commerce launch.
- A unique carpet is a one-off inventory item; stale availability creates a material trust risk.

## Working principles

- Do not invent history, provenance, scarcity, or technical attributes.
- Show the real carpet before asking the customer to trust the seller.
- Explain expert knowledge in natural Persian.
- Start with the customer's room and decision, not internal market categories.
- Document meaningful decisions, evidence, and verification—not every keystroke.
