# Portfolio Case Study Draft

## Hosseintalab — Taking a Tehran carpet business online

**Status:** In progress  
**Role:** Product manager, product designer, frontend engineer, brand and content lead  
**Market:** Iran  
**Product:** Persian-first handwoven-carpet commerce experience

## Summary

I am leading the end-to-end digital transformation of Hosseintalab, a multigenerational carpet business based in Tehran's carpet bazaar. The business had strong offline reputation and export experience but no coordinated online presence.

The goal is not simply to publish an online catalog. The product must make an expert, high-consideration purchase understandable to ordinary home buyers while preserving the authority of the family business.

## The challenge

Handwoven carpets are unique inventory items with specialist terminology, variable dimensions, condition differences, and high purchase risk. A customer furnishing an apartment may know the room and color they need but not understand weaving origins, patterns, construction, or fair comparison.

At the same time, a premium visual identity can easily become cold, theatrical, or museum-like—working against the needs of a residential customer.

## Key decisions

### Build two distinct commercial paths

I separated the storefront into `فرش برای خانه` and `فرش آنتیک`. Residential buying begins with room, size, color, and budget; antique acquisition begins with authenticity, age, condition, restoration, and direct specialist consultation.

### Start with the customer's room

The interface lets customers begin with reception room, living room, bedroom, or small-space needs before introducing specialist terminology.

### Use real market categories

I separated the catalog into size, weaving origin, and pattern. This preserves real Persian-carpet vocabulary without mixing unrelated dimensions in one menu.

### Make consultation part of the product

Customers can begin by sharing a room image, approximate dimensions, colors, and budget. The seller's expertise becomes a usable service rather than a generic heritage claim.

### Balance premium and approachable

Early visual exploration became too dark and mysterious. I redirected the product toward warm residential editorial commerce: distinctive enough to be memorable, but friendly enough to sell carpets for real homes.
